Under the surface / proposed native architecture

Two inputs.
One living image.

Entirely on your Mac.

Small components. Hard boundaries.

  • CaptureCoordinator: permissions, source-process resolution, tap/device lifecycle, route and sample-format changes. Never silently capture all apps.
  • Audio callback: bounded copy only. No heap allocation, disk I/O, blocking lock, FFT, or UI work.
  • AnalysisEngine: worker-owned FFT buffers, silence gate, normalization, timestamps, discontinuity resets.
  • SceneEngine: reusable feature-to-material mappings; scene-specific geometry. UI state on the main actor, not in the audio callback.
  • StateStore: metadata health and audio health separately. UserDefaults for preferences; bounded artwork cache. Audio is never persisted.

A useful contract, not a raw firehose.

The renderer reads the newest immutable analysis snapshot. If it is stale, fade to rest instead of replaying an old pulse. Keep sample time and display time in one host-clock domain.

What makes it actually musical?

Proposed DSP / not a fixed animation timeline
01 / Measure

Read real samples.

Respect the actual sample rate. Compute stereo energy without phase-canceling a naïve L+R mix. Exclude other apps’ notifications.

02 / Separate

Find the texture.

Start with a 2,048-sample Hann-window FFT and a 512-sample hop. Group power into log bands, bounded by the Nyquist frequency.

03 / Stabilize

Follow, don’t flicker.

Adaptive normalization with a noise floor; ~20 ms attack / ~180 ms release as starting points. Silence must not get amplified into false movement.

04 / Compose

Give each sound a role.

Bass → scale; mids → deformation; highs → fine detail. Spectral-flux onsets add restrained accents. Onsets are not guaranteed beats or BPM.

60 fps

Proposed 1080p Apple Silicon target. 16.7 ms frame budget; adapt particle count, resolution, and bloom.

50–100 ms

Initial wired/local reaction-lag goal, including analysis. Measure end to end; never infer latency from frame rate.

30 fps

Battery / thermal preset. Stop invisible rendering and release capture when the user stops the session.

Zero uploads

Short, bounded in-memory buffers. No audio files, listening-history analytics, or cloud processing.

Synchronization is part of the product, not a polish item.

At 48 kHz, a 2,048-sample window spans ~42.7 ms before other delays. Use host timestamps, measure audible-to-visible lag, and tune smoothing accordingly. Bluetooth, AirPlay, lossless, and Spatial Audio need route-specific checks. Don’t claim a universal sync offset; don’t delay Music’s playback just to make the visualizer work.

Lifecycle is a first-class feature.

Music quits or pauses → release into stillness. Output route or format changes → stop, rebuild safely, flush old features, revalidate signal. Sleep/wake or permission revocation → reconnect or show a clear recovery state. Quit → destroy tap and aggregate device without altering Music’s playback.

Keep the first release honest.

Proposed distribution: local development first, then a signed/notarized direct-download app. App Store sandbox and automation requirements need separate validation. ScreenCaptureKit may be assessed as another capture adapter, but is not a promised protected-content fallback. No private now-playing APIs or DRM extraction.

The reality check / evidence & tradeoffs

Yes to the idea.
One gate before a promise.

A creative visualizer is buildable. Accurate visualization of your subscription playback depends on whether the supported capture path supplies usable, permitted samples on your Mac.

Documented capabilities

The building blocks exist.

  • Core Audio process taps can capture outgoing audio from selected processes; Apple’s sample instructions require macOS 14.2+.
  • MusicKit supports Apple Music catalog/library integration and authorized playback—not public decoded subscription PCM for an FFT.
  • Apple events support scriptable-app integration. We’ll inspect your installed Music dictionary for metadata, transport, and artwork availability.
  • Metal and Accelerate are a natural fit for native rendering and local signal processing.
Unverified / blocks the promise

Your tracks. Your route.

Apple’s tap documentation does not guarantee that every protected Apple Music stream, downloaded subscription track, format, or output route is available to capture.

Permission is not a DRM override. If the supported APIs don’t provide usable audio, we cannot honestly call a metadata-driven animation a live music visualizer.

This proposal has not captured Apple Music. The current workspace is Linux, not a Mac with your subscription.

The first thing to approve: a feasibility spike.

Gate, not a full implementation commitment

  1. Check the real environment.

    Confirm your Mac, macOS version, listening route, and desired distribution. Inspect Music’s scripting dictionary; request the two permissions with clear purpose strings.

  2. Establish an unprotected control.

    Tap a known local audio fixture. Verify valid PCM, sample rate, channel count, timestamps, expected band energy, source isolation, and uninterrupted speaker/headphone playback.

  3. Try actual subscription playback.

    Check streamed and downloaded Apple Music tracks on your real route. Start with local stereo output, then assess your Bluetooth/AirPlay and lossless/Spatial Audio setup. A downloaded subscription file is still protected.

  4. Make a go / no-go decision from evidence.

    Pass only if samples correlate with the audible music and metadata follows the correct source. If not, diagnose permission, process, route, and silence. If protected playback remains inaccessible, stop and ask you to revise scope—no silent fallback.

If the gate fails, the alternatives are different products.

Unprotected local files can provide an analyzable signal. A clearly labeled ambient mode can respond to artwork or track changes, but not honestly to the live spectrum. Apple’s built-in visualizer is the immediate alternative. None is secretly substituted for the Apple Music companion you asked for.

Why not just use MusicKit for whatever is already playing?

On native macOS, ApplicationMusicPlayer is an in-app player with independent state. SystemMusicPlayer is not a native-macOS global now-playing bridge; do not confuse its Mac Catalyst availability with a native Mac API. For a companion to the already-running Music app, the proposed bridge is Music’s public scripting interface. Catalog enrichment through MusicKit can be added later, with appropriate authorization.

Exactly which permissions and credentials are involved?

The companion proposes NSAppleEventsUsageDescription for Music automation, the Hardened Runtime Apple Events entitlement, and NSAudioCaptureUsageDescription for system-audio capture. The system displays its own prompts. No Apple ID password is collected; Music continues to handle your subscription. The preferred tap path does not request microphone access or screen images.

Local development and signed/notarized distribution have different signing needs. Apple Developer Program membership for distribution is separate from your Music subscription. Mac App Store sandbox/automation feasibility must be assessed independently; shipping there is not promised.

Why not ScreenCaptureKit, a microphone, or a virtual audio driver?

ScreenCaptureKit can deliver audio samples, but adds a different permission model and does not promise access to protected content. A microphone hears the room, fails with headphones, and is less private and less accurate. A virtual driver adds setup and routing complexity and is not a DRM solution. Start with the supported, audio-specific Core Audio tap and stop if the real-Mac evidence does not support the requirement.

What does “proper visualization” mean here?

Musical changes must alter the correct visual dimensions: low-end hits expand the geometry, melodic energy deforms it, and high frequencies add detail. Silence settles, pauses don’t produce invented beats, and route interruptions do not leave stale pulses running. Analysis uses actual audio—not elapsed track time, genre tags, or fabricated BPM.

Performance and sync figures are proposed acceptance targets, not benchmarks. After you approve the design, we’ll agree a concrete test strategy, including reference tones, impulses, silence, route recovery, real subscription tracks, and visual/accessibility checks before the full implementation plan.
